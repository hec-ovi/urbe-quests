# CONTRACT: quests/dialog

Purpose: assembles what an NPC is allowed to know into cache-ordered dialog context layers, asks the model for the NPC's spoken reply (whole or streamed, with companion offers), and remembers completed exchanges with tiered summarization.

## In
`new DialogContextService(input)` ([DialogContextService.ts](DialogContextService.ts)):
- `world`: [DialogWorld](schema.ts), Naming output or a world whose districts and parcels have no names yet; `meta.naming.theme` is required; `meta.setting`, when the world has one, says plainly what the city is and how life runs in it, and stands in the world segment in place of the bare theme word. `closed?` lists the parcels whose buildings nobody can go into (no interior was built); a person does not name them among the places around them. With Atlas `streets` (and `meta.gridAngle`), parcel `lot` and `access` and stop `position`s, places carry their street and nearest corner ([StreetNames](../world/streets.ts)) and a person knows where they stand; without them, places read as before. `types`: NPC type set ([../world/types/named-world.ts](../world/types/named-world.ts)).
- `sim`: `SimulationPort` (instances, behavior, liveness). The instance's optional `gender`, `age` and `traits` feed the person lines, and its `job` or `transitJob` the work line.
- `llm`: `LLMPort`, used only to fold old conversation turns into memory notes.
- `memory?`: `{ tailSize?, foldSize? }` (defaults 12 and 6).

Then:
- `attachQuestline(runtime)`: a `QuestlineRuntime` whose cast personas, flag-gated facts, active wants and endings join their NPC's context. Attaching a questline of the same id again replaces the earlier runtime, so a host that restores state per turn never stacks copies.
- `contextFor(npcId, timeMin, { guide?, events?, prior?, look?, here?, people?, task?, call?, addresses?, overheard?, carry? }) -> DialogContext`. `carry` is [DialogCarry](schema.ts) `{ credits, means, items: [{ name, from?: 'stranger', atMin? }], dealings?: [{ what, amount?, name?, atMin }], asked? }`, what the person has on them now as the host keeps it: whole credits, how their household stands for money (`short`, `getting-by`, `comfortable`, `well-off`), the things they carry besides their access cards (the address segment tells those), each the player gave them marked `from: 'stranger'` with when, what passed between them and the player told from the person's side (`gave-credits` and `gave-thing` they gave the player, `got-credits` and `got-thing` they got from the player, `lent`, `lifted` the player caught lifting from their pockets, `bribe-refused` money they refused to look the other way) and a sum they asked for and were not paid. `overheard` is [DialogOverheard](schema.ts)s `{ name, role?, player, reply }`, oldest first: what a person along with the player (following or leading them) heard the player say to other people and those people answer. `addresses` is [DialogAddresses](schema.ts) `{ home?, work?, here?, access?, caught?, ways? }`, by the host's numbering of its furnished buildings: `home` and `work` are [DialogAddress](schema.ts)es `{ parcelId, floor?, unit?, room? }` (the floor as its numbers count it, 0 the ground floor and below it basements; the dwelling or private room on it, `apartment 1407`, `office 305`; the kind of room in words), `here` where the person stands now inside a furnished building, `access` the [DialogAccess](schema.ts) cards they carry `{ parcelId, opens, tie? }` (what each opens in words, in which building, their `home`'s or their `work`'s) and `caught` how many times they caught the player lifting one; `ways` the [DialogWay](schema.ts)s `{ what: 'home' | 'work' | 'quest' | 'named', name?, metres, point, minutes, lift? }` the host measured from where the person stands: the walk to their home, their work, the place the player's story points to and an address the talk named, the compass point it lies toward, the minutes on foot and a lift ride `up` or `down` at the end. `call` is a [DialogCall](schema.ts) `{ caller: 'player' }`: the player is not with the person but called them, and they talk on the phone. `task` is a [DialogTask](schema.ts) `{ kind, place? }`, what the person is doing for the player now as the host runs it: `following` them, `leading` them (to `place`), `brought` them there (to `place`, arrived together), or an errand they asked for: `walking` (to `place`), `waiting`, `sitting`, `home`, `work`. `guide` is a [DialogGuide](schema.ts) `{ placeId, kind: 'parcel' | 'stop' | 'street' | 'person' | 'spot', name?, notes? }` (a street by its `StreetNames` id; a person the NPC walked the player to, by npcId, and a spot inside a building, a lift, the stairs, a room or a floor, both told by `name`): the place this NPC has led the player to, what the player sees it called, and plain scene sentences the host shows there. `events` are [DialogEvent](schema.ts)s the host saw happen near where the NPC stands, in the order the host gives them: `struck` `{ atMin, parcelId, metres, hard?, self?, down? }`, a car hit someone by that building that many metres away (fast, the NPC itself, the person still lying there), or `scene` `{ atMin, parcelId, metres, notes }`, a quest scene standing there since `atMin`, with the host's plain sentences of what shows. `prior` is the [DialogLine](schema.ts)s `{ speaker: 'player' | 'npc', text, atMin? }` the host showed in this conversation since the last recorded exchange, oldest first: authored openings, story choices and their replies, greetings. They follow the remembered turns, the NPC's without cues. `people` is a [DialogPeople](people.ts) the host builds with `peopleKnown` (on both entries, no node API) over its own simulation: the people this NPC works with, lives with and lives beside (six coworkers and three neighbours at most, family and anyone the player's line names always), each with the name they go by, their tie to the NPC, their work and hours, and where they are now as the host knows it (here in sight, at a place, at home, out, riding, or unknown for someone the host holds away from their day), and `unknown`, the words of the player's line that name somebody this NPC does not know. `look` is a [DialogLook](schema.ts), what the person looks like in plain words the host takes from the look it draws: `height`, `build`, `face` features, `hair`, `skin`, `eyes`, `wearing` (each garment, top to toe, with its colours) and `fabric`. `here` is a [DialogHere](schema.ts) `{ x, z, parcelId?, floor?, light?, building? }`: where the person stands in world XZ, the building and floor they are inside, the light there in the host's words, and `building`, a [DialogBuilding](schema.ts) from the host's interior data: each floor's shared rooms by kind and apartment numbers, the kind of room the person is in, how many lifts and staircases join the floors, and who the host sees inside now (a name when the person knows them, what they are, their floor and room).
- `recordExchange(npcId, { line, reply, atMin, prior? }) -> Promise<void>`: stores the `prior` lines, each at its own `atMin` or else the exchange's, then the player turn and the NPC turn at `atMin`, the NPC's without cues, before it returns. When the tail overflows it folds the oldest window into a digest note through the LLM; the promise settles when that fold is done and rejects with the provider error, or `E_LLM` when the note is empty. Folded turns leave the tail only once their note exists, and a failed fold keeps them for the next exchange to retry. Hosts call it after a reply completes, never for a failed or abandoned one, and catch the promise off the reply path.
- `recordOverheard(npcId, { name, role?, lines, atMin })`: this person was there when the player talked to somebody else (`name`, `role` their post or kind, `lines` the [DialogLine](schema.ts)s said, `npc` that other person's, oldest first) and remembers it as one note in their own memory digest, marked as other people's words with who spoke (`Overheard, not said to you: you were there when the player talked to Mira Chen, the receptionist. The player said: "…" Mira Chen said: "…"`), the newest 4 lines without cues, each cut at a word to 160 characters. While the same talk goes on its note stays the newest and is written again with the new lines; anything else remembered in between starts a new one. The memory keeps the minute as `heardAtMin`.
- `serializeMemory()` / `restoreMemory(data)`: [MemorySnapshot](schema.ts)s `{ digest, turns, heardAtMin?, life? }` by npcId; a restored `life` is kept as it was.

`new Converse(llm)` ([Converse.ts](Converse.ts)), `llm` an `LLMPort` or a [StreamingLLMPort](../ports/llm.ts):
- `reply({ context, name, line }) -> Promise<string>`: the NPC's cleaned spoken reply to the player's typed `line`. The system prompt is the context segments in order with how to answer ([reply.md#rules](prompts/reply.md)) between the ones that stay the same from turn to turn and the ones that change (from `conversation` on), so a prompt cache keeps everything up to the talk so far; the turn's message is the line ([reply.md#line](prompts/reply.md)). The prompt carries the conversation on, as briefly as people talk and without saying again what the NPC said unless asked: a player who seems lost gets the matter the NPC raised, explained in character, and the day, hour and place stay background. It lets the reply carry inline emotion cues from [CUES](../flow/cues.ts), sparingly and where they happen.
- `replyStream({ context, name, line, offers?, signal?, turns? }) -> AsyncGenerator<ReplyEvent>`: the same reply over `llm.stream`, one OpenAI-compatible request with `stream: true`. `offers` is `{ follow?, places?: [{ placeId, name, relation? }], walk?, stop?, home?, work?, wait?, sit?, contact?, meet?: { name }, give?, take?, credits?, sell?, buy? }`, what the host lets this NPC agree to: the actions it may take for the player (`relation` what a place is to the person, `home`, `work`, a `venue`...) and what may change hands ([offers.ts](offers.ts)): `give` and `take` `{ items: [{ itemId, name, copy? }] }` (what the person may hand the player, a card as a `copy`; what the player may hand the person), `credits` `{ carried, purse, offered? }` in whole credits (what the person carries, what the player has, a sum the player holds out now; a line that holds credits out, `heldOut`, sets `offered` for the turn when the host gave none), `sell` and `buy` `{ items: [{ itemId, name, price }] }` (what the person sells at their counter at its price; the player's things the person may buy, at the most they would pay). Whenever the NPC may agree to anything the request carries every action tool, the same for every person and turn (`offerTools`), because model servers render tools at the very start of the prompt; what it may do now, with the places and cards by id, goes in the turn's message (`offerListing`, [offers.md#available](prompts/offers.md)), and a call outside it is refused. The offers instructions join the system prompt beside reply.md's rules, followed by the trade rules ([offers.md#instructions-trade](prompts/offers.md)), fixed text, the same every turn. `turns` is the conversation so far, oldest first, up to the line ([DialogLine](schema.ts)s): what the person offered or was asked a moment ago is read from its last exchange ([agreement.ts](agreement.ts) `pendingOf`) and said at the end of the message (`pending-lead`, `pending-offer`, `pending-asked`, and for things and money `pending-asked-item`, `pending-offered-item`, `pending-offered-buy`, `pending-asked-amount`, `pending-offered-amount`). The tools: `come_along`, `take_them_to({ placeId })` and, with `walk`, `walk_to({ placeId })` over `places` (buildings, streets, people, spots inside a building), `stop` (stop following, leading or waiting for the player), `go_home`, `go_to_work`, `wait_here` and `sit`, `give_number` with `contact` (the player may call the person from now on; as private as their home), `meet_them` with `meet` (on a call, come to where the player is, `meet.name`) and `give_item({ itemId })` with `give` `{ items: [{ itemId, name }] }` (hand the player a copy of one of the access cards the person holds; their home's is more private than their home, their work's staff or service card their employer's trust, never for a stranger who just asks), or one of the things the person carries, which leaves them. The trade tools, listed in the message only while what they need is on the table: `take_item({ itemId })` with `take` (take what the player hands them), `give_credits({ amount })` while they carry credits (pay the player from what they carry), `take_credits({ amount })` while credits are held out (take them), `ask_credits({ amount })` with `credits` (name a sum they want), `sell_item({ itemId })` with `sell` (sell one thing from their counter at its price) and `buy_item({ itemId, amount })` with `buy` (buy one of the player's things); `amount` is an integer from 1 to 1000. Each call is held to the table: `give_credits` no more than `carried`, `take_credits` no more than `offered` or `purse`, `sell_item` at the listed price whatever was said, `buy_item` for the sum named or else the price, no more than the price or, with `credits`, what they carry; anything else is refused. The trade rules tell the person that money is credits, that they have on them only what they are told they carry and never give or promise more, that a gift from a stranger is a favour entered against them, how disposition and means weigh (hostile takes and gives nothing, wary asks what it is for first, short of money a few credits matter), that they may pay someone for help from what they carry, and that an official offered money to look the other way is offered a bribe, which most refuse and the few who take never say aloud. [offers.md](prompts/offers.md) tells the NPC that a player who asks one of them has consented, so agreeing is calling the one tool that does it, and that it happens once the two of them are done talking, which the NPC says in its own words; whether it goes is its own decision from its disposition, the talk so far and the request: its home only for someone it likes or trusts, its work or a public place more readily, never off its post on duty; hostile refuses rudely, wary asks why or refuses, friendly agrees. It declines in character, in words only, and calls no tool the player did not ask for; words that agree are agreeing, so it calls the tool in the same reply, and an offer of its own the player accepts it carries out with take_them_to to that place, never an errand of its own; it goes only to the places the message lists. Words and actions agree in code too ([agreement.ts](agreement.ts) `inferOffers`): a reply that agrees in words alone (`stanceOf`) to what the player's line asks (`askedOf`: a thing handed over, credits held out, money asked for, an order at a counter and a thing offered for sale, read ahead of the rest so "here, take this card" hands a card over; then a card, one of the things the person carries asked for by its name, follow, a lead or walk to a place the line names by its words, number or what it is to the person, a number, home, work, wait, sit, stop) makes that offer, and a thank-you takes what was handed or held out; a sum is read by `amountIn`, digits or one to fifty or a hundred in words next to a money word or within 20 characters after a word that pays, never an address, a count or a time ("floor 14", "apartment 1407", "5 minutes"); what the person offers of their own accord changes hands only through a tool call, never on words alone; a player who accepts (`accepts`: "ok", "I follow you", "lead the way") what the person offered a moment ago gets that offer unless the reply refuses, in place of a walk or follow the model called for an errand of its own; and a reply that says it leads the way to a place it or the line names makes the lead. Only what `offers` allows. `signal` aborts the request. A port without `stream` yields the whole `reply` as one delta and offers nothing.

Both use `context.characterName` when supplied, otherwise the caller's `name`, so an authored character identity stays consistent between the context and the reply request. This is optional free chat; it never progresses a quest. Essential quest conversations use authored offline text and explicit replies through [QuestlineRuntime.dialogueFor / chooseDialogue](../flow/CONTRACT.md), without calling this service or a model. Context segments describe model grounding and are not player-facing NPC speech.

`cleanReply(text, names?)` and `new ReplyCleaner(names?)` with `push(text)`, `end()` and `done` ([ReplyCleaner.ts](ReplyCleaner.ts)): the one cleaner every reply passes through. It drops `<think>` blocks and `<|...|>` template tokens (a start token before the reply drops its role line; a token after the reply ends it), a `Name:` or `assistant:` tag in front of a line, everything from a `Player:` or `User:` line on, and one pair of quotes wrapping the whole reply, and trims. A reply that opens with a quote, after any cues, is held until that quote closes; the pair goes only when the closing quote ends the reply or only cues follow it. A bracketed tag is up to 32 letters, spaces, commas, apostrophes and hyphens in single or doubled brackets. A tag whose first or last word is a cue or a form of one becomes that cue as its lower-case name (`[Sighs heavily]` and `[[sigh]]` become `[sigh]`, `[a bitter laugh]` becomes `[laugh]`). Any other tag, a stage direction such as `[leans in]`, goes with the whitespace before it, and the text it joins is scanned again, so the reply holds no tag outside the list. `done` turns true once the model has written past the NPC's turn. Streamed pieces join to exactly the whole-text result: a tag split across model chunks, and text a dropped tag may still join, waits until settled, so no piece splits a cue. Memory notes pass only its markup stage, [cleanMarkup](../ports/markup.ts), so a note keeps any speaker labels it has.

## Out
`DialogContext`: ordered `segments`, each `{ id, text, shared }`, in fixed order world, type, npc, address, quest, memory, conversation, overheard, place, events, people, turns: what stays the same from turn to turn first, what changes last. Address is present only with `addresses`; quest is omitted when this NPC has no attached quest knowledge; memory is omitted when the digest is empty; conversation only once something has been said, overheard only with `overheard`, place only with a `guide`, events only with `events`, people only with `people`. `shared: true` segments (world, type) are byte-stable across calls and across NPCs of a type: the engine concatenates segments in order and may place provider cache breakpoints after shared ones.
- world: the person as a resident whose life the layers are, judged by everyone they meet on whether they are a real person: specific and grounded in their own life, short by default, mundane details over atmosphere, no stock phrases or narration; speaking from their own life and the conversation alone, never inventing events, people, places or family and saying so as themselves when they do not know; suspicion of a stranger kept to what a resident would think (an official, an informant); the dark tone and what they may want ([prompts/dialog-system.md](prompts/dialog-system.md)), the named districts, and the theme.
- address (with `addresses`, after npc): their home and work by address, the narrowest part first (`apartment 201, floor 2, Blockhouse Elin, an apartment block on Second Street ...`), to say as they are when asked; the doors they carry cards for, each at its building and whether it is their home's or their work's, which stay locked to anyone without a card unless someone lets them in, and that they can hand the player a copy if asked and they decide to; and, when they caught the player lifting a card, that they do not trust them.
- npc: their life so far ([LifeHistory.ts](LifeHistory.ts) `lifeHistory`, items in [prompts/life.md](prompts/life.md)): where they grew up, the work they did before and do now, two or three things that stay with them, what they care about (the people they live with first), one thing they keep to themselves and how they feel about the city by their disposition, every choice drawn from their npcId and no relative the household lacks; told once and kept in their memory (`life`), so it never changes after. Before it, the deterministic background: name with gender and age, traits, what they look like (with `look`, told as the truth to give when asked), home (with its street and nearest corner, and for a real apartment its number and floor), work and shift (a building with its street, a stop or station, or the transit lines), who they live with from the simulation's household (`you live alone: no partner and no children`, or each member by tie and name and what they do not have, as their whole household, never to be added to), haunts, their day today stretch by stretch (asleep, home, work, out, each with its place and hours), how they take to strangers (`dispositionOf` their traits and type category: hostile, wary, civil or friendly), plus quest personas.
- quest: for a person any attached questline casts, who the player is: the first attached questline's `prologue`, framed as how the player came in, background to what has happened since and the talk at hand; none when no questline has one. Then facts whose gate flag is set, the active steps this NPC wants (what happens and what it means to them), each active talk step with this NPC that carries authored dialogue in the NPC's own words (its opening, its answer to each question and, as the player's choice, the answers that would settle it, never a settling reply), and the epilogue of an ending this NPC's questline reached.
- place: the guided place by name and kind of building, stop or street with its district, whether this NPC works, lives (their own apartment by number and floor) or spends free time there (from its own job and routine), and the host's scene notes; a person or a spot inside a building the NPC walked the player to is told by its name, a person as standing there with them both.
- events: what happened around the NPC, one line each: the building named as places are, the distance (`right where you stand` under 15 m, else to 10 m) and how long ago (a moment, minutes, an hour, hours, a day, days), a person still down, and a scene's notes; it is on the NPC's mind, and the player may not know.
- people: the people this NPC knows, one line each (name, tie, work and hours, where they are now and whether the player is asking about them), framed as all it knows of anyone: it never makes up where somebody is, when they work, what they do or how they are tied to it, and a shared family name means nothing unless this says so; then, for each name the player used that it does not know, that it knows nobody by that name and says so.
- conversation: the verbatim tail and the `prior` lines as one conversation. It only grows between folds, so a prompt cache keeps it.
- overheard (with `overheard`): the player's talks with other people this person heard, each with who the player talked to (`Mira Chen, the receptionist`), what the player said and what they answered, marked as other people's words, not said to this person, which they may tell when asked.
- turns: the volatile now line (day, time and what the NPC is doing, in plain words), with `call` that the player is talking to them on the phone, not with them, with `task` what it is doing for the player and why; with `here`, where they stand (the street, the nearest corner and the district), the building they are inside with its floor or stand outside, with `addresses.here` where inside by address (`on floor 2, in apartment 201, in the living room`, also on a call), and with `building` the room they are in, what each floor holds (floors alike told together, apartments by their numbers), the lifts and staircases and who is in the building now with their floor and room, the places within 100 m by name and kind with distance and compass point (six at most, stops and stations included, no building the world lists `closed`), and the light; where their day takes them next (the end of the walk they are on, else the next place and when); and with `addresses.ways` how far each place lies (a `quest` way only to a person an attached questline casts) (`your home: about 350 metres to the north-east, 5 minutes on foot, then the lift up`, `right here` under 20 m), to say when asked and never to guess another distance. With `carry`, last: what they have on them right now and nothing else (their credits in Bureau notes, or no money at all, and each thing, one the player gave them with how long ago), their household's means in plain words, what passed between them and the player with how long ago, and a sum they asked for and were not paid; nothing of it in any other segment, so the start of the prompt stays the same whatever they carry.

Places are named as the world names them; an unnamed building or stop is its kind (`an apartment block`, `a subway station`) and an unnamed district its tier and kind (`a poor industrial district`). No segment carries a district, parcel, stop or route id.

`ReplyEvent`, in order: `{ type: 'delta', text }` as cleaned text arrives, cues included; then, once the spoken reply is complete, one `{ type: 'offer', kind }` per distinct valid offer from the model's tool calls: `follow`, `stop`, `home`, `work`, `wait`, `sit` or `contact`, `lead` or `walk` with `placeId` and `name`, `meet` with `name`, `give` or `take` with `itemId` and `name`, `pay`, `accept` or `ask` with `amount`, `sell` with `itemId`, `name` and `price`, or `buy` with `itemId`, `name` and `amount`; one offer per thing and each money offer once (`offerKey`), however many calls one reply makes; then `{ type: 'done', reply, offers }` with the whole cleaned reply. The deltas join to `reply`. A host shows `stripCues` of a whole reply, a sentence or the text so far, and hands the raw text to a voice. A turn that fails yields no offer. When the model called tools and said no words, each call gets a tool result and a second request over the same tools streams the spoken reply (its calls are not taken), a space after any cue the first answer gave; a call whose arguments are not JSON goes back with `{}`. A call naming a tool or place the host did not offer is dropped. Once the model writes past the NPC's turn, the stream stops reading it. An offer is the NPC's agreement; the host decides whether anything happens.

The context retains its actual `npcId` and optionally carries `characterName { given, family }` from that person's attached cast role. The NPC background projection and explicit character prompt use that authored name consistently; the stored simulation name, body identity, schedules, relationships and memory keys are unchanged. The first attached matching named role supplies the presentation name. Unnamed roles and bystanders retain their generated identities.

## Errors
- `E_WRONG_STATE`: contextFor on a dead NPC (the dead do not talk).
- `E_UNKNOWN_ID`: NPC type missing from the type set.
- `E_LLM`: a reply with no spoken words left after cleaning (cues alone are none), or an empty memory note.
`SimulationError` from context queries, provider exceptions and aborts pass through. `recordExchange` stores the supplied NPC ID without a Simulation lookup.

## Invariants
- Closed knowledge: a person knows the player's business only as a story casts them and from what they overheard (`recordOverheard`). Context text contains only world rules, type boilerplate, simulation background, attached personas, who the player is for a cast person, unlocked quest facts, this NPC's active wants, its own authored talk lines and lived endings, the guided place and its host notes, what the host saw happen nearby, and recorded or shown conversation. Scope is decided by runtime state and the cast mapping, never by the model; gated facts with unset flags and other NPCs' wants never appear.
- Shared segments are memoized per service (the cache for common instances) and stable per world/type.
- The LLM summarizes memory, writes replies and may make offers; code selects context facts and which offers exist. Model grounding still requires evaluation with the selected provider.
- Words and actions agree: a reply that agrees in words to what is on the table makes its offer even when the model called no tool (`inferOffers`).
- No talk prompt names the machinery behind the person: the person in front of them is `they`/`the stranger`, the transcript says `Them:` and `You:`, and the tools are `come_along`, `take_them_to`, `walk_to`, `stop`, `go_home`, `go_to_work`, `wait_here`, `sit`, `give_number`, `meet_them`, `give_item`, `take_item`, `give_credits`, `take_credits`, `ask_credits`, `sell_item` and `buy_item`. Text written outside the talk (memory notes from before, a world's type boilerplate, an authored persona) passes `plainWords` first. [grounding.ts](grounding.ts) checks prompts and replies: `voiceProblems` (the words of the machinery, `META_WORDS`, and stock genre phrases, `STOCK_PHRASES`) and `groundingProblems(reply, context)` (an incident outside an everyday phrase, a relative or roommate, living alone against a household, or a capitalised name the context does not give), and `moneyProblems(reply, carry)` (a sum the reply hands over or promises, "here's 50", "I'll give you 20", "take 10 credits", beyond what the person carries).
- Prompts and tool descriptions live in [prompts/](prompts/): dialog-system.md, background.md, life.md, context.md, summarize.md, reply.md, offers.md. reply.md lists the cues from `CUES`. A running host reads each section from `dist` on every call, so section ids are part of the interface and keep their names. No output caps.
