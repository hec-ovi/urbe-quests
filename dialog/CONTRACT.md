# CONTRACT: quests/dialog

Purpose: assembles what an NPC is allowed to know into cache-ordered dialog context layers, asks the model for the NPC's spoken reply (whole or streamed, with companion offers), and remembers completed exchanges with tiered summarization.

## In
`new DialogContextService(input)` ([DialogContextService.ts](DialogContextService.ts)):
- `world`: [DialogWorld](schema.ts), Naming output or a world whose districts and parcels have no names yet; `meta.naming.theme` is required. With Atlas `streets` (and `meta.gridAngle`), parcel `lot` and `access` and stop `position`s, places carry their street and nearest corner ([StreetNames](../world/streets.ts)) and a person knows where they stand; without them, places read as before. `types`: NPC type set ([../world/types/named-world.ts](../world/types/named-world.ts)).
- `sim`: `SimulationPort` (instances, behavior, liveness). The instance's optional `gender`, `age` and `traits` feed the person lines, and its `job` or `transitJob` the work line.
- `llm`: `LLMPort`, used only to fold old conversation turns into memory notes.
- `memory?`: `{ tailSize?, foldSize? }` (defaults 12 and 6).

Then:
- `attachQuestline(runtime)`: a `QuestlineRuntime` whose cast personas, flag-gated facts, active wants and endings join their NPC's context. Attaching a questline of the same id again replaces the earlier runtime, so a host that restores state per turn never stacks copies.
- `contextFor(npcId, timeMin, { guide?, events?, prior?, look?, here?, people?, task? }) -> DialogContext`. `task` is a [DialogTask](schema.ts) `{ kind, place? }`, what the person is doing for the player now as the host runs it: `following` them, `leading` them (to `place`), or an errand they asked for: `walking` (to `place`), `waiting`, `sitting`, `home`, `work`. `guide` is a [DialogGuide](schema.ts) `{ placeId, kind: 'parcel' | 'stop' | 'street' | 'person' | 'spot', name?, notes? }` (a street by its `StreetNames` id; a person the NPC walked the player to, by npcId, and a spot inside a building, a lift, the stairs, a room or a floor, both told by `name`): the place this NPC has led the player to, what the player sees it called, and plain scene sentences the host shows there. `events` are [DialogEvent](schema.ts)s the host saw happen near where the NPC stands, in the order the host gives them: `struck` `{ atMin, parcelId, metres, hard?, self?, down? }`, a car hit someone by that building that many metres away (fast, the NPC itself, the person still lying there), or `scene` `{ atMin, parcelId, metres, notes }`, a quest scene standing there since `atMin`, with the host's plain sentences of what shows. `prior` is the [DialogLine](schema.ts)s `{ speaker: 'player' | 'npc', text, atMin? }` the host showed in this conversation since the last recorded exchange, oldest first: authored openings, story choices and their replies, greetings. They follow the remembered turns, the NPC's without cues. `people` is a [DialogPeople](people.ts) the host builds with `peopleKnown` (on both entries, no node API) over its own simulation: the people this NPC works with, lives with and lives beside (six coworkers and three neighbours at most, family and anyone the player's line names always), each with the name they go by, their tie to the NPC, their work and hours, and where they are now as the host knows it (here in sight, at a place, at home, out, riding, or unknown for someone the host holds away from their day), and `unknown`, the words of the player's line that name somebody this NPC does not know. `look` is a [DialogLook](schema.ts), what the person looks like in plain words the host takes from the look it draws: `height`, `build`, `face` features, `hair`, `skin`, `eyes`, `wearing` (each garment, top to toe, with its colours) and `fabric`. `here` is a [DialogHere](schema.ts) `{ x, z, parcelId?, floor?, light?, building? }`: where the person stands in world XZ, the building and floor they are inside, the light there in the host's words, and `building`, a [DialogBuilding](schema.ts) from the host's interior data: each floor's shared rooms by kind and apartment numbers, the kind of room the person is in, how many lifts and staircases join the floors, and who the host sees inside now (a name when the person knows them, what they are, their floor and room).
- `recordExchange(npcId, { line, reply, atMin, prior? }) -> Promise<void>`: stores the `prior` lines, each at its own `atMin` or else the exchange's, then the player turn and the NPC turn at `atMin`, the NPC's without cues, before it returns. When the tail overflows it folds the oldest window into a digest note through the LLM; the promise settles when that fold is done and rejects with the provider error, or `E_LLM` when the note is empty. Folded turns leave the tail only once their note exists, and a failed fold keeps them for the next exchange to retry. Hosts call it after a reply completes, never for a failed or abandoned one, and catch the promise off the reply path.
- `serializeMemory()` / `restoreMemory(data)`.

`new Converse(llm)` ([Converse.ts](Converse.ts)), `llm` an `LLMPort` or a [StreamingLLMPort](../ports/llm.ts):
- `reply({ context, name, line }) -> Promise<string>`: the NPC's cleaned spoken reply to the player's typed `line`, asked from the context segments joined in order as the system prompt plus [prompts/reply.md](prompts/reply.md). The prompt carries the conversation on, as briefly as people talk and without saying again what the NPC said unless asked: a player who seems lost gets the matter the NPC raised, explained in character, and the day, hour and place stay background. It lets the reply carry inline emotion cues from [CUES](../flow/cues.ts), sparingly and where they happen.
- `replyStream({ context, name, line, offers?, signal? }) -> AsyncGenerator<ReplyEvent>`: the same reply over `llm.stream`, one OpenAI-compatible request with `stream: true`. `offers` is `{ follow?, places?: [{ placeId, name }], walk?, stop?, home?, work?, wait?, sit? }`, what the host lets this NPC agree to: the actions it may take for the player. Each adds its tool: `follow_player`, `lead_player_to({ placeId })` and, with `walk`, `walk_to({ placeId })` over `places` (buildings, streets, people, spots inside a building), `stop` (stop following, leading or waiting for the player), `go_home`, `go_to_work`, `wait_here` and `sit`. [offers.md](prompts/offers.md) tells the NPC that a player who asks one of them has consented, so agreeing is calling the one tool that does it, and that it happens once the two of them are done talking, which the NPC says in its own words; whether it goes is its own decision from its disposition, the talk so far and the request: its home only for someone it likes or trusts, its work or a public place more readily, never off its post on duty; hostile refuses rudely, wary asks why or refuses, friendly agrees. It declines in character, in words only, and calls no tool the player did not ask for. `signal` aborts the request. A port without `stream` yields the whole `reply` as one delta and offers nothing.

Both use `context.characterName` when supplied, otherwise the caller's `name`, so an authored character identity stays consistent between the context and the reply request. This is optional free chat; it never progresses a quest. Essential quest conversations use authored offline text and explicit replies through [QuestlineRuntime.dialogueFor / chooseDialogue](../flow/CONTRACT.md), without calling this service or a model. Context segments describe model grounding and are not player-facing NPC speech.

`cleanReply(text, names?)` and `new ReplyCleaner(names?)` with `push(text)`, `end()` and `done` ([ReplyCleaner.ts](ReplyCleaner.ts)): the one cleaner every reply passes through. It drops `<think>` blocks and `<|...|>` template tokens (a start token before the reply drops its role line; a token after the reply ends it), a `Name:` or `assistant:` tag in front of a line, everything from a `Player:` or `User:` line on, and one pair of quotes wrapping the whole reply, and trims. A reply that opens with a quote, after any cues, is held until that quote closes; the pair goes only when the closing quote ends the reply or only cues follow it. A bracketed tag is up to 32 letters, spaces, commas, apostrophes and hyphens in single or doubled brackets. A tag whose first or last word is a cue or a form of one becomes that cue as its lower-case name (`[Sighs heavily]` and `[[sigh]]` become `[sigh]`, `[a bitter laugh]` becomes `[laugh]`). Any other tag, a stage direction such as `[leans in]`, goes with the whitespace before it, and the text it joins is scanned again, so the reply holds no tag outside the list. `done` turns true once the model has written past the NPC's turn. Streamed pieces join to exactly the whole-text result: a tag split across model chunks, and text a dropped tag may still join, waits until settled, so no piece splits a cue. Memory notes pass only its markup stage, [cleanMarkup](../ports/markup.ts), so a note keeps any speaker labels it has.

## Out
`DialogContext`: ordered `segments`, each `{ id, text, shared }`, in fixed order world, type, npc, quest, memory, place, events, people, turns. Quest is omitted when this NPC has no attached quest knowledge; memory is omitted when the digest is empty; place is present only with a `guide`, events only with `events`, people only with `people`. `shared: true` segments (world, type) are byte-stable across calls and across NPCs of a type: the engine concatenates segments in order and may place provider cache breakpoints after shared ones.
- world: character-play, register, dark-tone, conversation-thread, people-are-only-what-the-layers-say and deflection rules ([prompts/dialog-system.md](prompts/dialog-system.md)), the named districts, and the theme.
- npc: the deterministic background: name with gender and age, traits, what they look like (with `look`, told as the truth to give when asked), home (with its street and nearest corner, and for a real apartment its number and floor), work and shift (a building with its street, a stop or station, or the transit lines), family, haunts, their day today stretch by stretch (asleep, home, work, out, each with its place and hours), how they take to strangers (`dispositionOf` their traits and type category: hostile, wary, civil or friendly), plus quest personas.
- quest: for a person any attached questline casts, who the player is: the first attached questline's `prologue`, framed as how the player came in, background to what has happened since and the talk at hand; none when no questline has one. Then facts whose gate flag is set, the active steps this NPC wants (what happens and what it means to them), each active talk step with this NPC that carries authored dialogue in the NPC's own words (its opening, its answer to each question and, as the player's choice, the answers that would settle it, never a settling reply), and the epilogue of an ending this NPC's questline reached.
- place: the guided place by name and kind of building, stop or street with its district, whether this NPC works, lives (their own apartment by number and floor) or spends free time there (from its own job and routine), and the host's scene notes; a person or a spot inside a building the NPC walked the player to is told by its name, a person as standing there with them both.
- events: what happened around the NPC, one line each: the building named as places are, the distance (`right where you stand` under 15 m, else to 10 m) and how long ago (a moment, minutes, an hour, hours, a day, days), a person still down, and a scene's notes; it is on the NPC's mind, and the player may not know.
- people: the people this NPC knows, one line each (name, tie, work and hours, where they are now and whether the player is asking about them), framed as all it knows of anyone: it never makes up where somebody is, when they work, what they do or how they are tied to it, and a shared family name means nothing unless this says so; then, for each name the player used that it does not know, that it knows nobody by that name and says so.
- turns: the volatile now line (day, time and what the NPC is doing, in plain words), with `task` what it is doing for the player and why; with `here`, where they stand (the street, the nearest corner and the district), the building they are inside with its floor or stand outside, and with `building` the room they are in, what each floor holds (floors alike told together, apartments by their numbers), the lifts and staircases and who is in the building now with their floor and room, the places within 100 m by name and kind with distance and compass point (six at most, stops and stations included), and the light; where their day takes them next (the end of the walk they are on, else the next place and when); then the verbatim tail and the `prior` lines as one conversation.

Places are named as the world names them; an unnamed building or stop is its kind (`an apartment block`, `a subway station`) and an unnamed district its tier and kind (`a poor industrial district`). No segment carries a district, parcel, stop or route id.

`ReplyEvent`, in order: `{ type: 'delta', text }` as cleaned text arrives, cues included; then, once the spoken reply is complete, one `{ type: 'offer', kind }` per distinct valid offer from the model's tool calls: `follow`, `stop`, `home`, `work`, `wait` or `sit`, or `lead` or `walk` with `placeId` and `name`; then `{ type: 'done', reply, offers }` with the whole cleaned reply. The deltas join to `reply`. A host shows `stripCues` of a whole reply, a sentence or the text so far, and hands the raw text to a voice. A turn that fails yields no offer. When the model called tools and said no words, each call gets a tool result and a second request without tools streams the spoken reply, a space after any cue the first answer gave; a call whose arguments are not JSON goes back with `{}`. A call naming a tool or place the host did not offer is dropped. Once the model writes past the NPC's turn, the stream stops reading it. An offer is the NPC's agreement; the host decides whether anything happens.

The context retains its actual `npcId` and optionally carries `characterName { given, family }` from that person's attached cast role. The NPC background projection and explicit character prompt use that authored name consistently; the stored simulation name, body identity, schedules, relationships and memory keys are unchanged. The first attached matching named role supplies the presentation name. Unnamed roles and bystanders retain their generated identities.

## Errors
- `E_WRONG_STATE`: contextFor on a dead NPC (the dead do not talk).
- `E_UNKNOWN_ID`: NPC type missing from the type set.
- `E_LLM`: a reply with no spoken words left after cleaning (cues alone are none), or an empty memory note.
`SimulationError` from context queries, provider exceptions and aborts pass through. `recordExchange` stores the supplied NPC ID without a Simulation lookup.

## Invariants
- Closed knowledge: context text contains only world rules, type boilerplate, simulation background, attached personas, who the player is for a cast person, unlocked quest facts, this NPC's active wants, its own authored talk lines and lived endings, the guided place and its host notes, what the host saw happen nearby, and recorded or shown conversation. Scope is decided by runtime state and the cast mapping, never by the model; gated facts with unset flags and other NPCs' wants never appear.
- Shared segments are memoized per service (the cache for common instances) and stable per world/type.
- The LLM summarizes memory, writes replies and may make offers; code selects context facts and which offers exist. Model grounding still requires evaluation with the selected provider.
- Prompts and tool descriptions live in [prompts/](prompts/): dialog-system.md, background.md, context.md, summarize.md, reply.md, offers.md. reply.md lists the cues from `CUES`. A running host reads each section from `dist` on every call, so section ids are part of the interface and keep their names. No output caps.
