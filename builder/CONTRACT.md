# CONTRACT: quests/builder

Purpose: translates a story arc into a validated questline: a text-only plan pass answers who does what, where the story splits, what each part means and where the artifacts enter, and closes with a manifest of ids; the build loop commits exactly that manifest to the flow tools; roles are cast against the simulation by type.

## In
`new QuestlineTranslator().translate(input)` ([QuestlineTranslator.ts](QuestlineTranslator.ts)):
- `assignment`: `QuestAssignment { title, synopsis, characters, arc }` ([schema.ts](schema.ts)), the slice of story to translate.
- `world`, `types`; `sim`: `SimulationPort`; `ports { plan: LLMPort, build: AgentPort }` ([../ports/llm.ts](../ports/llm.ts)); `parcels?`: the buildings the story may use; `mechanics?`: the step kinds the host can play, every kind when omitted; `scenery?`: the scenery the host stages ([capabilities](../handoff/schema/host-capabilities.schema.json)), none when omitted.
- `referenceTimeMin?` (cast resolution time, default Tuesday 10:00), `maxRounds?` (overrides the plan's budget), `planned?` (told the `PlanResult` once the plan parsed, before the build starts), `progress?` (one `BuildProgress` per build round: title, round, maxRounds, committed and planned pieces, the tools called or what happened instead, and the round's refused calls as the agent read them).

The halves run alone: `new TranslationPlanner().plan({ assignment, world, types, llm, mechanics?, scenery? }) -> PlanResult { text, manifest }` ([TranslationPlanner.ts](TranslationPlanner.ts)) and `new QuestlineBuilder().build({ assignment, plan, manifest, world, types, sim, agent, mechanics?, scenery?, ... }) -> BuildResult` ([QuestlineBuilder.ts](QuestlineBuilder.ts)).

The mechanic allowlist ([mechanics.ts](mechanics.ts)): `playableKinds(mechanics?)` gives the playable kinds in catalog order and throws on a list naming no real kind. The planner's vocabulary line (`{{mechanics}}`), the step catalog, the item prose saying which steps tell information (`{{informs}}`) and the builder's tools are all rendered for those kinds: `stepCatalog(kinds)` keeps only their `## <kind>` sections, and `builderTools(kinds)` ([tools.ts](tools.ts)) narrows the target `kind` enum, offers only the target fields those kinds name and describes only their lines. `BUILDER_TOOLS` is `builderTools()`, every kind. An `add_step` of a real kind outside the list is answered `error: step kind <kind> is not playable here; use one of <kinds>`, before its schema is checked.

Staged scenes: with `scenery`, the plan is told the city stages what a story leaves in a place and says what each scene the story has leaves there, and `builderTools(kinds, scenery)` adds `stage_scene` (`stageSceneTool(scenery)`, [tools.ts](tools.ts)). Its place kinds, poses, prop kinds and limits are the ones the host declares, and its description lines name only those. A call is a [staging](../handoff/CONTRACT.md#stagings): what the player finds in the story's words, the step that stages it and whether from its opening or once it is done, the step whose building it stands in, its people, marks and items, which element shows each clue, and how it clears. A staging is refused while it has a [staging problem](../handoff/CONTRACT.md#stagings), names a step, role or item the manifest lacks, or stands at a step already in that happens in no building; one staged again under its sceneId replaces it. With `scenery`, `finish_questline` also requires every investigation step's clue on a staged scene and checks the scenes the finished questline stages through the handoff's scenery, investigation and host capability audits. Without `scenery` the agent gets no staging tool or prompt, and investigation steps take their scenes from the handoff input.

## Out
`TranslationResult { plan, definition, cast, scenes }`: the plan text (manifest included), a `QuestlineDefinition` that passed `FlowValidator` ([../flow/schema.ts](../flow/schema.ts)), its `ResolvedCast`, and the stagings it stages in the order they were first staged (empty without `scenery`). A staging names steps, not buildings, so it follows its steps wherever casting pins them.

`StoryVenues` ([StoryVenues.ts](StoryVenues.ts)) finishes the questline before it validates: each role's meeting place moves to a building that publishes a post for that character (the staffing role read from the type's own words, else its category, against the [venue table](../world/venues.ts)) and, when the role's steps name an hour, publishes that post; every parcel that role's steps named moves with it. A building published for residents and guests alone hires nobody, so it is never a story venue. Then every authored place gets the venue's name and every step whose text names an hour gets its window ([../flow/StepStamp.ts](../flow/StepStamp.ts)). `new StoryVenues(world, types, parcels?)` takes the buildings the story may use: venues are chosen from that set, a place outside it moves to a building of the same kind inside it (its own district first, then world order), and `outside(definition)` names whatever still falls out, by the step or item that names it. `StoryVenues.pin(definition, posts)` finishes it after the cast: a step moves for the people it meets (the role a `talk` names, both roles a `listen` names, or the character a step is wanted by while the step stands at that character's own meeting place), only to the building where that character holds a post, and only when everyone the step meets agrees on one. Everything else stays where the story put it.

`CastResolver.cast(definition, referenceTimeMin, options?)` ([CastResolver.ts](CastResolver.ts)) first casts at the hour the story meets each role (the start of the first window its own steps name, on the reference day when that day has it, else the reference time): a living, unassigned person of that name already in the world, then whoever holds the post at the pinned venue and the other buildings that publish it. If those posts cannot supply an unassigned person at that minute, it checks the remaining hours of the authored window, then day, evening, night and staggered shift hours across the recurring week. Opening a game after closing time therefore still finds its day staff. Only after those post queries does it reserve a named identity or use an unassigned living person of the type already in the world. The simulation says where the city hires ([Workplaces.ts](Workplaces.ts)): a parcel it staffs nobody in answers "nobody" and is never asked again, and no reservation is given a job there. `options.taken` holds the people already playing a part and `options.characters` the characters already cast, so one person is one character across a set of questlines; only the same role id and type deliberately reuse that character. An exhausted cast is blocked rather than assigning another character's person, and a blocked questline commits neither set.

`CastResult { cast, posts, blocked? }`: `cast` is `roleId -> npcId`; `posts` is `roleId -> the building that character was found holding a post in`, absent for a role filled from an existing person or any other fallback; `blocked` is `{ roleId, npcType, reason }` when nobody can play a role, and then `cast` and `posts` hold the roles filled so far. Casting reads the questline and never rewrites it: `QuestlineBuilder` and `UniqueCast` pin with `posts` while the questline is built, so the bundle ships the final places and the runtime reads them.

The manifest ([PlanManifest.ts](PlanManifest.ts)): the plan's last section, `## Manifest` with one line each of `roles:`, `items:`, `acts:`, `endings:`, `steps:` listing machine ids (a kind in parentheses is ignored, `none` is an empty list). `parsePlanManifest(plan)` reads it; a plan without a usable one gets one repair round ([prompts/translate-plan-repair.md](prompts/translate-plan-repair.md)). Facts are not planned: the builder adds them freely.

Agent-facing surface: `builderTools(kinds)` ([tools.ts](tools.ts)) with narrative-first schemas: items carry a kind (device, weapon, document, key, substance, valuable, information); steps carry wantedByRoleId, a stake, gives and needs. Its target catalog is the playable part of the flow contract's 16-kind vocabulary, all of it by default. Places use one catalog `parcelId`, `districtId`, `stationId`, or `stopId`. Investigation, rescue, escort, access, hacking, sabotage and transportation carry their exact evidence, cast, credential, route, target, journey and completion identities. A `lead-player` escort ends at a building, station or stop, where its character can walk the player; one to a district is refused. Every call is checked against its own schema first ([checkToolInput.ts](checkToolInput.ts)), so a half-written one, or a list longer than its tool allows, comes back naming the fields it needs; a target missing a field its kind needs comes back the same way with the line saying what that kind takes, and so does a `simFlag` effect without `roleId` or `op`. The target's fields are declared in the one order every kind's line names them, a place after the ids it is the place of and the completion flag last, so a model writing a target along its schema never passes a field it still needs. A call refused again word for word (in any key order) for the same reason comes back with [prompts/refused-again.md](prompts/refused-again.md) appended. `WorldTargetAudit` rejects unknown NPC types and world identities before a role, item, or step enters the draft. The draft is bound by the manifest: an id the plan does not list is refused with the planned list, a piece added again under an id already in replaces it (so a problem `finish_questline` reports is fixed with the same tools), every role and item referenced by any target is checked against the manifest the moment it is made (all problems in one message), each success reports `n of m planned pieces in`, and `finish_questline` names the planned pieces still missing before it validates. Problems return as tool results the agent corrects, never as aborts.

## Errors
- `E_LLM`: no usable manifest after the repair round (detail: stage, raw, problems), the agent answered in words instead of tools more than three times (each such reply is answered with [prompts/builder-nudge.md](prompts/builder-nudge.md) naming what is still missing, and the loop goes on), or the round budget ran out; the message carries the committed count.
- `E_CAST`: a role has no castable NPC. The build raises it with the `CastBlock` in `detail`; a published questline carries the same reason in `blocked` instead.
Other `SimulationError`s pass through.

## Invariants
- The plan asks for at least six steps and two acts in a main line, and at least four steps in a side situation. Step, act and branch counts have no upper bound.
- Tool execution is process-bounded at two rounds per planned manifest piece plus eight. `maxRounds` may override this safety budget; it never limits the manifest or model output length.
- The planner sees the arc, the cards, the world brief and the step catalog of the playable kinds (no ids); the builder sees the plan, the cards, the synopsis, the same catalog and the parcel, district, station, and stop catalog with ids, never the arc. Neither sees a kind the host cannot play.
- The manifest is the bound: the finished questline carries exactly the planned roles, items, acts, endings and steps, plus whatever facts the agent added.
- The agent never sees or emits NPC ids or coordinates; roles bind types, the simulation resolves people. The script owns personality, needs, drives and voice; the simulation owns home, job, family and routine.
- `add_role.characterName` preserves the script's given and family name for dialog and player labels without requiring a reserved identity. `reservedName` remains the separate request for a fixed simulation identity; the presentation name never influences cast selection.
- A published step and its cast name the same place: the built questline is pinned to the buildings its own people hold posts in, and a role filled off a post moves no step.
- Flags referenced by drafted steps and facts are auto-declared; the finished definition always satisfies the flow validator.
- Scenes are staged only where the story has them; every figure, mark and item in one comes from the staging. A quest character stands in a scene only dead, after a step kills them. The agent stages in the host's vocabulary only.
- Prompts live in [prompts/](prompts/), including tool descriptions under `prompts/tools/` and the staging text for plan and build ([prompts/staging.md](prompts/staging.md)). Step-catalog examples read want, cost, then change. Minimums are floors, story breadth has no upper count, and model output has no token, word or character cap.
