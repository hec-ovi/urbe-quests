# Changelog

0.29.1: The reply checks read people as they talk: an incident word in an everyday phrase (a place to crash, killing time, fire away) is no incident, a curly apostrophe or none ("I’m", "Ive") is no name, and a reply that lives alone while the household holds somebody, or names a roommate the household lacks, is flagged. A person who lives with others no longer cares most about being left alone. The live check finds the model on the machine the container runs on with `LIVE_TALK_URL=host`.

0.29.0: A person in a talk passes for a resident with a life of their own. Every person gets a life history told once from their simulation record and kept in their memory (`life`): where they grew up, the work they did before and do now, two or three things that stay with them, what they care about (the people they live with first), one thing they keep to themselves and how they feel about the city, every choice drawn from their id and no relative their household lacks. The world rules say they are judged by everyone they meet on whether they are a real person: specific, grounded in their job, household, day and history, short by default, no stock phrases or narration, no invented events, people, places or family, and a stranger may be an official or an informant. No talk prompt names the machinery behind them: the person in front of them is "they" or "the stranger", the transcript says `Them:`, the tools are `come_along`, `take_them_to` and `meet_them`, memory notes are folded in the second person, and older notes, type boilerplate and personas pass `plainWords`. A world's `meta.setting` says what the city is in place of a bare theme word; events are framed as background to mention only when asked or relevant; three places nearby, for directions. `voiceProblems` and `groundingProblems` check prompts and replies; a test renders ten lines to five people and a snapshot of one whole request, and with `LIVE_TALK_URL` checks a live model's replies. The creation sample's name pool has ordinary names in place of genre nicknames.

0.28.0: A person knows the player's business only as a story casts them and from what they were there to hear. `recordOverheard(npcId, { name, role?, lines, atMin })` keeps an exchange somebody overheard in their own memory as one short note of that talk, marked as other people's words with who spoke, the newest four lines cut to 160 characters, written again while the same talk goes on; `MemorySnapshot` carries `heardAtMin`, the minute they last overheard the player. A `quest` way is told only to a person an attached questline casts.

0.27.0: What a person says and does agree, and talk prompts keep their start for the prompt cache. `inferOffers` reads a reply that agrees in words alone ("Fine", "Sure", "Here") to what the player's line asks (follow, a lead or walk to a place named by its words, number or what it is to the person, a number, a card, home, work, wait, sit, stop) and makes that offer; a player who accepts ("ok", "I follow you", "lead the way") a place the person offered a moment ago ("Want me to walk you there?") is led there, in place of an errand the model called for its own; `replyStream` takes the conversation so far as `turns` and says at the end of the message what is pending. Whenever a person may agree to anything, the request carries every action tool, the same each turn, and what the person may do now with its places and cards by id goes in the message; how to answer and how to agree join the system prompt between the layers that stay and the ones that change, the talk so far is its own `conversation` segment ahead of them, and the spoken follow-up after a tool-only answer keeps the same tools: in a measured six-turn talk the prompt a cache must process each turn falls from about 1,550 tokens to about 375. Offer places carry their `relation`. A person's background says who they live with from the simulation's household, or that they live alone with no partner and no children, as their whole household, and the reply rules forbid inventing family, people at home or places. `addresses.ways` says how far home, work, the story's place and a named address lie, with the walk, the compass point, the minutes and a lift; `overheard` tells a person along with the player what they heard the player say to others, marked as other people's words; a world's `closed` buildings are no places around to name; the offers prompt forbids making up a shop or a corner to head for.

0.26.0: People know their addresses and the doors they hold cards for, and stories can lock a door. `contextFor` takes `addresses` (`DialogAddresses`): the person's home and work by address (the building, the floor as its numbers count it, the dwelling or private room on it), where they stand now inside a furnished building, the access cards they carry and how often they caught the player lifting one; a new `address` segment after `npc` says them, and the turns segment says where inside they stand. `replyStream` offers take `give` (`{ items: [{ itemId, name }] }`), the `give_item({ itemId })` tool, an offer of kind `give`, to hand the player a copy of one of their cards; the offers prompt holds a home's card more private than the home and a work's card as the employer's trust. A quest item of kind `key` or `device` may carry `opens` (`{ parcelId, door }`), the locked door it is an access card for, and an `access` step may name a `door` by its address, which the host's lock plays with no mission asset binding; the builder's `add_item` and `access` target offer both.

0.25.0: A person can give the player their number and talk on the phone. `replyStream` offers take `contact`, the `give_number` tool (an offer of kind `contact`), which the offers prompt holds as private as the person's home: a friendly person gives it, anyone else only once they have come to trust the player; and `meet` with the place's name, the `meet_player` tool (an offer of kind `meet` with that name), to come to where the player is on a call. `contextFor` takes `call` (`DialogCall`, `{ caller: 'player' }`), and the turns segment says the player called them and is not with them, so they talk as people do on the phone and say where they are when it matters.

0.24.0: A leader who has arrived with the player knows it: `task` kind `brought`, with the place, says they are there together instead of still on the way.

0.23.0: A person knows what they are doing for the player. `contextFor` takes `task`, as the host runs it: following the player, leading them to a place, or an errand they asked for (walking somewhere, waiting, sitting, going home or to work), and the turns segment says it beside the hour and what their day has them doing.

0.22.0: An NPC's tools are a set of actions it may take for the player, each offered only when the host allows it: `follow_player`, `lead_player_to` and `walk_to` a place, a street, a person or a spot inside a building, `stop`, `go_home`, `go_to_work`, `wait_here` and `sit`. The offers prompt tells the NPC to decide each by its disposition, the talk so far and the request, to call the one tool that does what was asked, and to say in its own words that it does it once the talk is done; each call is an offer event of its kind.

0.21.0: A person inside a building knows the building. `here.building` carries what the host's interior data says: the room the person is in, each floor's shared rooms and apartment numbers, the lifts and staircases, and who is inside now with their floor and room; the turns segment tells it, floors alike together. A guide may be a person the NPC walked the player to or a spot inside a building (a lift, the stairs, a room, a floor), told by name, and a leader at its own home reads its apartment's number and floor while the host's notes say where exactly it stands. The companion tools name people and such spots among what a player may ask to be taken to.

0.20.0: A person knows themselves and where they stand. `contextFor` takes `look`, what the person looks like in the host's plain words (height, build, face, hair, skin, eyes, each garment with its colours, the fabric), and the npc segment tells it as the truth to give when asked. The home reads with its street and nearest corner and, for a real apartment (`home.apartment`), its number and floor; the work reads with its street; the day today is told stretch by stretch with places and hours; and each person takes to strangers as `dispositionOf` their traits and type category says, hostile, wary, civil or friendly. `contextFor` also takes `here`, where the person stands, and the turns segment says the street and corner, the district, the building they are in or outside, what lies within 100 m by name, distance and compass point, the light, and where their day takes them next. `StreetNames` names a grid city's streets from Atlas geometry (Streets from the north, Avenues from the west), `DialogWorld` carries Atlas `streets`, lots, access points and stop positions, a guide may name a street, and a leader at its own apartment knows it stands at its door. The companion tools take a request to be led to a place, a street, home or work as the NPC's own decision by its disposition and the talk so far, and an agreement sets off once the talk is done, which the NPC says. `dispositionOf` and `willingTo` are on both entries for a host that decides without a model. People know other people only as the host's simulation says: `peopleKnown` lists who a person works with, lives with and lives beside, with their work, hours and where they are now, and marks the ones the player's line names; a new `people` segment tells them, a name the player uses for somebody the person does not know reads as nobody they know, and the system prompt forbids making up anyone's whereabouts, hours, work or ties. A named character written as one gender, by the name pool's tag for the given name or else the persona's pronouns, is cast onto a person of that gender whenever one can play the part, so a woman in the script is drawn as a woman; the standalone stub draws a tagged name's gender too.

Player text: creation and gameplay adaptation enforce field reading budgets with repair paths; long journal prose remains available. Recorded quests introduce one lead at a time, with side jobs offered after their main-story context.
