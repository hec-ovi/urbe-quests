# The Water Bill

## Logline
When the lower floors of Blockhouse Elin flood on schedule and the drowned are filed as accidents, a barista who lost her brother, a struck-off medic and a sergeant with a vanished report pull on the same thread until it reaches the Helix Dynamics Tower. The city can keep the truth or sell it; either way the water keeps rising.

## Characters

### Mara Vex
Role: a barista at the Static Cafe in Kanaal Market
Background: Grew up in Blockhouse Elin in the Sump. Her brother Tomas drowned when the lower floors flooded last winter; the precinct called it an accident and the clinic called it pneumonia. She moved up to the Market to be near the people who decide things.
Want: Proof that the flood was scheduled, and a name to say it to. Fears becoming one more person who talks about it and does nothing. Would never take Helix money, which is why she has none.
Voice: Dry, short, jokes to keep distance. "Coffee's free if you're paying with bad news." "You want sugar or you want the truth? Only one of them is in stock."

### Doc Sanna
Role: a back-alley medic at Doc Sanna's clinic in the Sump
Background: Ran a Helix employee clinic in Crown Spire until she reported respiratory deaths in the tenements as tenement deaths, not employee deaths. Struck off within the month. Keeps a paper list of everyone the Sump has lost, because paper does not get patched.
Want: To keep the clinic supplied and the list intact. Fears the list being taken more than she fears being taken. Would never sign a form she has not read.
Voice: Tired, exact, no adjectives. "Bring me the bottle, not the story. Stories don't stop bleeding."

### Ilse Varga
Role: a corporate executive at Helix Dynamics Tower in Crown Spire
Background: Born in Slagrow Stacks in Rustfields, the only one of her family to cross the water. Signed the hydrological adjustment memo that lowered the Sump's flood barrier to protect the Tower's foundations, and told herself the tenements had been warned.
Want: To be right about the memo. Fears her own name on it more than the drowned. Would never go back to Rustfields, which is why the story takes her there.
Voice: Polished, sentences that end in questions so the other person owns the answer. "You understand the barrier protects everyone on this side of it, don't you?"

### Sergeant Dev Okoro
Role: a precinct officer at Precinct 9 in Kanaal Market
Background: Filed the flood report, complete with the barrier maintenance log, the night the water came. The report vanished from the precinct system by morning and his captain suggested he had never written it. He kept his own copy, which is a crime.
Want: The report on the record with his badge number on it. Fears losing the pension that keeps his mother in Blockhouse Vosk. Would never lose the copy.
Voice: Flat, procedural, then a sudden edge. "That is not a case, that is a weather event. Says so on a form I did not write."

### Kettle
Role: a street hustler in the Sump
Background: Nobody remembers Kettle's real name, including Kettle. Sells whatever falls off corpo trucks between the Grey Market Exchange and the Blockhouses. Pulled a Helix data chip out of the floodwater in Elin's stairwell along with a body, and has been trying to sell one of them since.
Want: One buyer, one price, then gone. Fears the chip being worth more than his life, which it is. Would never give it away, until the story asks.
Voice: Fast, every line a deal. "Half now, half when you're happy, and you'll be happy."

### Petra Lind
Role: a fabrication line worker at Rustfields Fab 3
Background: Rides the night bus from Slagrow Stacks to Fab 3 and back. The night of the flood she saw Helix maintenance drones over the Sump barrier at an hour nothing flies. She has told nobody, because nobody has asked.
Want: To be asked. Fears her shift supervisor, who is Helix's too. Would never lie about what she saw, which is different from volunteering it.
Voice: Quiet, literal, remembers times exactly. "Twenty past three. I know because the bus was late and I was counting."

## Presentation

### Rain on the Static Cafe
Kanaal Market at opening hour, neon still on under a grey sky, the canal high enough to lick the walkway. Mara Vex wipes the same counter for the third time while Sergeant Okoro takes his coffee at the end of it, uniform damp, saying nothing. He has been coming in since the winter, and in all that time he has ordered and paid and never once said her brother's name. Today a stranger comes in out of the rain and asks for anything hot. Mara pours. "Coffee's free if you're paying with bad news," she says, and Okoro sets his cup down a little too hard. The stranger has none, yet. By the time the cup is empty Mara has said Tomas's name for the first time in front of the sergeant, and Okoro has left without finishing, which he has never done.

### The List at Doc Sanna's
The Sump in daylight, stairwells still stained to the second floor. Doc Sanna's clinic is a ground-floor unit with the door propped open by a crate of expired saline. Sanna is stitching a hustler's forearm and does not look up. "Bring me the bottle, not the story," she tells him, and Kettle, holding his arm together, tells the story anyway: a chip in the stairwell, Helix stamped, wet but readable, and a buyer in Crown Spire who has stopped answering. Sanna finishes the stitch, opens a drawer, and shows the stranger a paper list with forty-one names. Tomas Vex is the ninth. "The precinct has a shorter list," she says. "I would like to know why." What was a barista's grief is now a medic's ledger, and a hustler's chip has a shape.

## Development

### The Copy in the Locker
Precinct 9 after shift change, the lockers lit by one working tube. Okoro has heard Mara say the name and cannot unhear it. He opens his locker and takes out a folder he has told himself for months does not exist: the flood report, the barrier maintenance log, a Helix work order with a signature. "That is not a case," he says, mostly to the folder, "that is a weather event. Says so on a form I did not write." He hands over one page, the work order, and keeps the rest. The signature reads I. Varga. The stranger leaves the precinct holding the first proof anyone outside the Tower has touched, and Okoro locks the door on his pension.

### Twenty Past Three
The night bus out of Rustfields, empty except for Petra Lind and the stranger. Petra recognizes the Helix work order's date before she recognizes the logo. "Twenty past three," she says. "I know because the bus was late and I was counting." Maintenance drones over the barrier, six of them, at an hour nothing flies. She will say it again to anyone who asks, she says, and then she looks at the window, and the stranger understands that nobody has. Kettle, meanwhile, has found his buyer's name on the same work order and has decided the chip is now worth exactly one life, and would like it to be somebody else's. He offers a deal: the chip, for a way out of the Sump. The story now has a witness, a document, a device and three people who cannot use them alone.

## Conflict

### Lunch at the Noodle Saint
Noodle Saint at the executive lunch hour, the good booths by the window. Ilse Varga is here because Crown Spire kitchens are owned by people who report to her, and she needs to hear herself think. In the next booth, unseen, the stranger listens as she takes a call about the barrier being lowered again before the spring tides. "You understand the barrier protects everyone on this side of it, don't you?" she says to the phone, and then, quieter, to herself, "I told them to warn the Blockhouses." She did not. The chip in the stranger's pocket holds the recording that proves it. When she leaves she looks straight at the booth, and something in her face says she already knows how this ends.

### The Barrier Yard
Rustfields at dusk, the barrier yard behind Fab 3 where the maintenance drones dock. Petra brought the stranger here on her way to shift; Kettle came for the money; Okoro came in uniform, which was a mistake. Helix security arrives before the drones do. What breaks is Kettle's nerve: he tries to sell the chip to the security lead on the spot and is refused with a broken hand. What it costs is Okoro's badge, taken at the gate by his own captain, who has been on the Helix work order all along. Petra records the whole thing on the Fab's line camera, because she counts things, and gives the stranger the only copy on a Fab 3 access key. The chip, the work order, the recording and the list are now in one pair of hands, and Ilse Varga has sent a car to the Static Cafe.

## Resolution

### The Offer at the Static Cafe
Closing time. Ilse Varga sits at Mara's counter, alone, coat still wet. She has not come to threaten. She has come to buy: the chip, the key, the list, at a price that would move the Blockhouses above the waterline, on her word. "That is a lot of money for a weather event," Mara says. Varga's answer is the first sentence she has spoken in years that does not end in a question: "I signed it." If the stranger sells, the Sump gets rebuilt three floors higher and the drowned stay filed as accidents, and Mara pours one last coffee for the woman who killed her brother and does not spill it. That is one ending, and nobody in it is wrong.

### On the Record
Or the folder goes across the counter the other way, to Okoro, who has no badge and no pension and a mother in Blockhouse Vosk, and who walks it into Precinct 9 with the stranger beside him and reads it aloud in the squad room until someone writes it down. Doc Sanna's list gets stapled to the back. The precinct opens a file that Crown Spire will spend a decade trying to close, and the barrier is not lowered before the spring tides because a fab worker who counts things is now, officially, a witness. Ilse Varga goes back to Rustfields, to Slagrow Stacks, in a car that does not wait for her. The water still rises. But the names are on paper now, and paper does not get patched.
