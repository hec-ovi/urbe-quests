# The Weir Line

## Logline
When crane operator Sable Voss drowns in a gantry "accident" timed to a water ration nobody admits to scheduling, his sister won't let the report close, and the thread she pulls runs from the flooded stalls of Sump Row to the boardroom glass of Aurelian Hydro. A clinic nurse who counts the drowned lungs nobody reports, an analyst who wrote the algorithm that timed the cut, and a guard who was told to lose the paperwork follow it with her. What they find is a ration starved on purpose to protect a quarter's numbers, and a choice about who gets to know why the harbour drowns on schedule.

## Characters

### Sable Voss
Role: a harbour crane operator on the Sump Row gantries
Background: Fifteen years on the cranes, long enough to know every freighter by the sound of its hull against the pilings. Raised his sister Petra alone after their mother's lungs gave out. The week the water ration dropped to its lowest tier in a decade, his gantry lost power mid-lift during a low tide nobody had scheduled, and he went into the sump with it.
Want: Wanted his sister clear of the docks before the rot took her too. Feared dying the way their mother did, slow and unlisted. Would never have called that lift a coincidence, which is why the record says he did.
Voice: Even-keeled, counted things aloud before he lifted them. "Four containers, one gantry, no surprises." He is not alive to speak in this script; his voice survives only in what the others remember of it.

### Petra Moss
Role: a renter in a Sump Row tenement block, Sable's sister
Background: Works the counter at a mall stall by day and files nothing by night, because filing means believing the report. Has kept every one of Sable's shift logs since their mother died, out of a habit that became evidence.
Want: A name on the accident, not a weather pattern. Fears that grief with no name attached just becomes weather too. Would never take a payout that reads like a price on him.
Voice: Blunt, runs out of patience before she runs out of words. "He counted the lifts. The city didn't count him."

### Kip Thorn
Role: a street kid working the stalls and gutters of Sump Row
Background: Slept two nights in the flooded stairwell under Dredge Feed before he found what the tide left behind: a company drive, waterlogged but readable, tangled in the same debris that took Sable Voss. Has traded worse things for less.
Want: The drive out of his hands before whoever wants it back finds out he has it. Fears being the kind of story the Sump forgets by winter. Would never hand it over for free, until someone asks him right.
Voice: Fast, transactional, softens when nobody's buying. "Everything's got a price till it's got a name on it. Then it's got a cost."

### Doc Mira Chen
Role: a clinic nurse at the Vitalis Spire Clinic in the Glass Apex
Background: Treats the burnouts and overworked execs by day for a wage; treats Sump Row's dock workers off the books after hours, in the storage room, for nothing. Has kept a private list of every dock worker who came in with drowned-sounding lungs since the last ration cut, because the clinic's official chart has no room for them.
Want: The list to matter to someone with the power to act on it. Fears it disappearing with her, unread. Would never turn away a patient who can't pay, which is the whole reason she is poor enough to still mean it.
Voice: Tired, exact, no wasted words. "I don't chart what I treat after hours. Doesn't mean I don't count it."

### Talis Rook
Role: a corporate analyst at Aurelian Hydro in the Glass Apex
Background: Wrote the model that schedules Sump Row's water allocation against Aurelian Hydro's quarterly hydro-power draw, and has spent two years telling herself the model is just math. The week Sable Voss died, the model recommended a cut it had never recommended before, and she signed off on it without reading past the summary line.
Want: To find out the cut was a coincidence. Fears finding out it was not, more than she fears losing the job that pays for that fear. Would never admit out loud that she already knows which it is.
Voice: Precise, then suddenly plain when the precision runs out. "The model doesn't lie. I'm less sure about the people reading it."

### Cross Ivanov
Role: a security guard at Apex Oversight in the Glass Apex
Background: Filed the maintenance report on Sable Voss's gantry the night it happened, power log and all. By morning his captain told him the file had never existed, and suggested he agree. He kept a copy anyway, in a locker he is not supposed to still have a key to.
Want: The report on record with his name on it, not just his conscience. Fears losing the pension that keeps his mother housed in Sump Row. Would never destroy the copy, which is the only rule he has not broken yet.
Voice: Flat and procedural until it isn't. "That's not an accident report. That's a form somebody wanted filled out fast."

### Ilona Vale
Role: a resident of the Glass Apex, married onto the Aurelian Hydro board
Background: Grew up in Sump Row before she married her way to the top floor, and has spent every year since deciding that counts as having earned the view. Signed the memo that let Aurelian Hydro cut the ration early to protect the quarter, and told herself the harbour has always flooded, memo or not.
Want: To be right about the memo. Fears her own signature more than she fears the drowned. Would never go back to Sump Row to check, which is why the story sends her there in every way but the one that matters.
Voice: Measured, ends her sentences like questions so the room agrees for her. "The ration protects the whole grid. You understand that, don't you?"

## Presentation

### The Lift That Wasn't Scheduled
Rain on the gantry line at low tide, the kind that comes early enough nobody's braced for it. Sable Voss takes the morning lift the way he takes every lift, counting the containers aloud into his radio, and the power drops mid-count. What the report will call a routine outage, the men on the next crane over call something else, quietly, once the ambulance stops being useful. By the time Petra Moss hears about it from a neighbor instead of the precinct, the file has already been marked weather-related, and she has already decided that it isn't.

### The Counter and the List
Sump Exchange mall, Petra's shift not over yet, her hands moving on autopilot while her head is somewhere in a filing office that won't take her calls. A regular she half-knows, a clinic nurse named Doc Mira Chen who comes in for cheap synth-tea between double shifts, sets a folded page on the counter without a word: a list of names, dock workers, all with the same drowned cough Sable had the week before he died. Petra doesn't recognize most of the names. She recognizes the pattern.

### What the Tide Kept
Dredge Feed after the sump has drained back to ankle height, market stalls reopening around the wreckage like nothing happened. Kip Thorn digs a company drive out of the same debris pile that took Sable Voss, the casing cracked but the core intact, and spends a full day deciding who's worth more to sell it to before deciding that's the wrong question. He finds Petra instead, because everyone in Sump Row already knows whose brother that debris belongs to. "Everything's got a price till it's got a name on it," he tells her. "Yours has a name on it."

## Development

### The Cup That Doesn't Cool
Aurelian Hydro's lobby floor, Talis Rook running the ration model again for the fourth time that week, checking a number that keeps coming back the same. She orders her coffee the way she always does, black, too hot to drink, and lets it go cold on her desk while she rereads the memo authorizing the cut, looking for the line that makes it someone else's decision. She doesn't find it. The signature at the bottom is Ilona Vale's, and Talis has met her exactly twice.

### The Copy in the Locker
Apex Oversight after shift change, the lockers lit by one working tube. Cross Ivanov has heard through the precinct grapevine that a Sump Row renter is asking questions the file was built to stop, and he has not been able to put the folder back in the drawer where it belongs since the morning his captain told him it didn't exist. He reads the maintenance log to himself one more time, the line between what he knows and what he is willing to lose finally thin enough to see through, and photographs the signature page before he can talk himself out of it.

### The View From the Top Floor
Ilona Vale's residence above the smog line, a dinner she is not eating because the call from Aurelian Hydro's legal desk started before the appetizer arrived. Someone is asking about the ration model's flagged anomaly on the week of the Sump Row drowning, and legal wants to know if that someone has a name yet. Ilona tells them no, which is true, and hangs up telling herself the harbour floods every year regardless of her signature, which she no longer entirely believes.

## Conflict

### What the Model Actually Said
Oxide Filter, the one coffee counter Talis trusts enough to think out loud near, though she never says anything out loud. A stranger she doesn't recognize sits close enough to hear the district's maintenance drones running an unscheduled pattern outside the window, six of them, redirecting power away from the Sump Row storm pumps at an hour nothing should be flying. Talis watches the same pattern on her own model's dashboard a floor up, in real time, and finally reads past the summary line. The cut wasn't a recommendation. It was a redirection, timed to protect the grid's numbers on the one night the harbour needed every watt it had.

### Dinner at Zenith Dining
The good booths by the window, executive hour. Ilona Vale is here because Aurelian Hydro's board eats where it can be seen eating, and today she needs an audience for a decision she has already made. In the next booth, unseen, someone hears her tell a fellow board member exactly what the redirection was for and exactly whose numbers it protected. "The ration protects the whole grid," she says, and then, quieter, not for the room: "I signed it knowing what it would do to the low tide. I didn't sign it knowing whose brother would be on the gantry." It is the closest she will ever come to confessing on the record, and someone finally has it on something better than memory.

## Resolution

### On the Record
Apex Oversight, late enough that the desk sergeant has stopped asking questions. Cross Ivanov lays out the maintenance log, the signature page, the model readout and Doc Mira's list on the counter in an order that makes itself into a case whether the precinct wants one or not, and reads it aloud until someone starts writing it down. He loses the pension inside the week. Petra Moss gets a name on the file at last, not weather, and Talis Rook gets called before an inquiry she does not try to avoid. Ilona Vale goes back to Sump Row exactly once, in a car that does not wait for her, and the ration schedule changes before the next low tide, not because anyone forgave her, but because now everyone is watching.

### The Payout
The Sump Row end of the block, closing time. Ilona Vale comes down from the Glass Apex herself, coat still dry because she took the covered walk, and offers Petra Moss a number that would move her whole block above the flood line, no admission attached, no name on any form. "That's a lot of money for a weather event," Petra says. Ilona's answer is the first sentence she has spoken all year that doesn't end like a question: "I know exactly what it was." If Petra takes it, the block gets rebuilt three floors higher and Sable Voss stays filed as an accident forever, and nobody who watches her sign the papers thinks she made the wrong choice.
