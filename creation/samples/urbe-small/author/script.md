# The Short Measure

## Logline
Nell Arden carries the water ration from Vantablack Dynamics to the Vitalis Spire Clinic every morning, and one wet Tuesday the sealed canisters weigh less than their seals say. Somebody between the corporate tower and the clinic is skimming the measure and selling it back to Sump Row by the cup. The proof is a stamped tally book at Weir Exchange, and the only question that matters is who hears about it first: the precinct that owes her a favour, the company that owns the numbers, or the street that has been paying twice.

## Characters

### Nell Arden
Role: a clinic courier and reception worker at the Vitalis Spire Clinic
Background: Grew up in Sump Row on half rations and learned to read a canister by its weight before she could read a label. Took the courier post because it came with a clinic badge and a clean coat, and she has carried the ration for three years without once opening a seal.
Want: To know the measure is honest, because the clinic's patients drink what she carries. Fears being the courier who looked away. Would never break a seal herself; she wants someone else to see it with her.
Voice: Quiet, precise, counts in grams. "Seal says twelve litres. My arms say ten and a half. My arms have never lied to me."

### Orrin Pike
Role: a ration clerk at Weir Exchange in Sump Row
Background: Twenty years stamping measures at the exchange counter. Pays a daughter's tuition in the Glass Apex on a Sump Row wage, which only adds up if the measure does not.
Want: One more quarter unnoticed, then out. Fears his daughter learning where the money came from. Would never sell to the clinic's own patients, a line he drew and keeps moving.
Voice: Tired charm, round numbers. "Water finds its level, love. I just help it along."

### Hale Mercer
Role: a precinct guard at Apex Oversight
Background: Walked a Sump Row beat before the Apex posting and still eats at the Rust Bucket Eatery when nobody from the tower is looking. Owes Nell for a night at the clinic nobody wrote down.
Want: A case clean enough to hand upstairs without it coming back on the street. Fears being the guard who arrests a clerk and protects a company. Would never take the tower's side just because it pays better.
Voice: Flat, slow, a pause before every promise. "Bring me paper. Paper I can walk up a staircase."

### Dessa Vey
Role: a vendor at the Sluice Grub stall in Sump Row
Background: Sells soup, filters and, lately, clean water by the cup from a barrel under the counter. Knows the barrel fills on Tuesdays and has decided not to ask why.
Want: Her regulars fed and her stall open. Fears the day the barrel stops coming. Would never overcharge a child, and would never say where the barrel comes from.
Voice: Warm and fast, a price in every sentence. "Cup's two, bowl's three, questions cost extra and I'm all out."

### Ivo Lann
Role: an analyst at Vantablack Dynamics in the Glass Apex
Background: Wrote the ration model that decides how many litres each clinic and district receives. Sees the shortfall as a rounding line on a report that nobody downstairs will ever read.
Want: A quarter that closes clean. Fears a story with his model's name on it. Would never falsify a figure, and would never check one he was not asked to.
Voice: Pleasant, hurried, always half somewhere else. "The model is correct. What happens to the water after it leaves the model is a logistics question."

## Presentation

### The Weight of Twelve Litres
Morning rain drums on the loading bay of the Vitalis Spire Clinic. Nell sets the day's canisters on the reception scale out of habit, the way her mother taught her, and the needle stops short of the stamped mark. She checks the Vantablack seal: unbroken, stamped at Weir Exchange that morning. She tries the second canister and the third. "Ten and a half," she says to nobody, then to the night nurse: "Every one of them."

### Soup at Sluice Grub
On her way back through Sump Row, Nell stops at the Sluice Grub stall for a bowl. Dessa Vey ladles it from a barrel that should not exist in a district on half rations. "Clean water, two a cup," Dessa says, and when Nell asks where it comes from she only smiles. "Comes on Tuesdays. Like the rain, only paid for." Nell sees the barrel carries the same stamp her canisters do.

## Development

### The Counter at Weir Exchange
Nell walks into Weir Exchange with a canister under her arm. Orrin Pike greets her by name, stamps a form without reading it and slides a tally book back under the counter. "Measure goes out full, love. What happens in the rain is the rain's business." She notices the tally book is the one thing on his counter he keeps his hand on.

### The Quarter's Numbers
Nell takes the question up to Vantablack Dynamics. Ivo Lann meets her in the lobby, reads her figures on his slate and smiles like a man declining a call. "A point three shortfall is inside tolerance. The model is correct." When she says the missing water is being sold in Sump Row, he looks at her for the first time. "Then that is a logistics question, and logistics is not me."

## Conflict

### The Tally Book
Nell finds a way to the tally book at Weir Exchange while Orrin is at lunch. Two columns in his hand: what the seals say and what went out, and between them a third, small and steady, marked with Dessa's stall and a dozen others. It is enough to hang a clerk and not enough to touch a tower. Orrin comes back early and sees the empty shelf. "You don't know what that book feeds," he says, and he does not raise his voice.

### Who Hears First
Nell sits in the rain outside the Rust Bucket Eatery with the book inside her coat. The precinct will take it and give her a clerk. The company will take it and give her nothing, cleanly. Sump Row will take it and lose the only clean water it has. Hale Mercer finds her there, as if by accident. "You look like somebody holding paper," he says. "Paper goes up a staircase or it goes into the harbour. Pick one."

## Resolution

### Paper Up the Staircase
If Nell gives the tally book to Hale at Apex Oversight, Orrin is taken off the Weir Exchange counter by the end of the week and the canisters come to the clinic full. Dessa's barrel stops. The tower sends a memo about tolerance. Nell weighs the next delivery and the needle reaches the mark, and she does not feel as good about it as she expected.

### Rain Keeps the Rest
If Nell keeps the book, she has something every side of the city wants, and a courier with leverage is still a courier. Orrin keeps stamping; Dessa keeps ladling; Ivo's quarter closes clean. Nell carries the ration every morning and weighs each canister on the reception scale, and writes the difference in a book of her own.
